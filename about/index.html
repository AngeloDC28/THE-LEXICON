About

Fashion shows, explained.

THE LEXICON explains 45 famous fashion shows from 1981–2023: what happened, why people still talk about them, where the ideas came from and what they went on to inspire.

Who makes it

THE LEXICON is researched and written by Angelo Sanchez Dela Cruz, a researcher and cultural analyst based in London. It is independent: no brand pays to appear, there are no ads, and nothing you do on the site is tracked.

Who it's for

Anyone curious about why clothes look the way they do, from a student starting an essay to a designer checking where an idea came from. Every show starts with a short, plain summary; the longer notes underneath go into the detail for people who want it. Stylists, costume teams and critics use it for references that hold up.

How each show is written up

  • A plain summary of what happened and why it matters.
  • Notes pinned to the photos, usually three per look, on what you are looking at.
  • Three longer notes: provenance (what happened), critique (what it means) and strategy (how it was made).
  • Sources for every show, so you can check the facts and cite them.
  • Tags on nine axes (designer, era, politics, theory, gender, materials, place, body and format) that link related shows in the Nexus.

The six big ideas

Every show is filed under one big idea. The plain name comes first; the theory name underneath is the one used in fashion writing.

  • Changing the body Corporeal Intervention
    Clothes that reshape, exaggerate or question what a "normal" body looks like.
  • Fashion vs. fashion Institutional Critique
    Shows that mock, or protest against, the fashion industry itself.
  • Street to runway Subcultural Codification
    Youth culture, queer scenes and protest turned into designer clothes.
  • Borders and belonging Strategic Appropriation
    Migration, home and identity: what you carry with you, and who decides.
  • Technology and the future Semiotic Sabotage
    Machines, screens and the planet: the body in a high-tech world.
  • Who is looking Historical Lineage
    The gaze and identity: who clothes are made to be seen by.
