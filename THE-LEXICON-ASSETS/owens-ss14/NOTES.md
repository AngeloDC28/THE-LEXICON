# owens-ss14
## Rick Owens SS 2014 — "Vicious"

### Collection Overview
- **Designer:** Rick Owens
- **Season:** Spring/Summer 2014
- **Show Date:** September 2013
- **Venue:** Palais du Trocadéro, Paris
- **Show Title:** "Vicious"

### Curatorial Notes
"Vicious" is the most discussed Rick Owens show and one of the most significant performances in the history of fashion-as-spectacle. In place of the conventional use of professional runway models, Owens cast members of seven American collegiate step-dance teams — a tradition rooted in historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs) and African American fraternities and sororities. The step-dancers replaced the neutral, controlled walking of the runway with an aggressive, percussive, communal performance form derived from a tradition entirely outside fashion's institutional frame.

The implications were multiple and have been extensively analysed. The replacement of the individualised, silhouetted model-walk with a collective, rhythmically interdependent performance foregrounded the body as a social and political entity rather than a neutral hanger. The step-dance tradition itself carries specific historical meaning — developed in African American communities, it is a practice of communal identity, discipline, and joy that fashion had never previously engaged with on its own terms.

The garments themselves were characteristically Owens: draped, asymmetric, in his signature palette of black, grey, bone, and dusty plum. Long leather skirts, deconstructed tunics, architectural draped tops, and the characteristic Owens footwear (platform boots, crepe-soled Rick Owens staples). But the garments were secondary in the show's logic — or rather, the show argued that clothing and body were inseparable, that garments only exist in motion and in relation to the bodies that wear them.

The casting was also a direct challenge to fashion's long-standing racial and body-type homogeneity. The step-dancers were diverse in size, shape, and build in ways that the runway almost never is. The show was immediately celebrated as radical; it has since become a touchstone for discussions of representation, cultural appropriation, and the politics of fashion spectacle.

### Target Looks 01–05

**Look 01**
The first step-dance team enters performing — the shock of the format is immediate. The opening look is likely a long, draped black or dark asymmetric top or tunic over a draped skirt or trouser in Owens's signature palette. The garment moves with the performance: the draping, the asymmetry, the fluid construction all become visible and alive in a way that the static runway suppresses.

**Look 02**
Second look in the sequence, worn by another dancer or group of dancers moving in coordinated formation. The palette continues: black, charcoal, or bone. The garment may introduce a leather element — a long leather skirt, a leather-trimmed top — that catches light as the dancers move.

**Look 03**
Mid-sequence. The step-dance performance has established its rhythmic and spatial logic. The third look likely introduces a variation in silhouette or material — a longer train, a wider drape, a garment that creates particular effects in motion (a flaring skirt, a swinging hem).

**Look 04**
Further development. Owens's palette in "Vicious" included dusty plum, dark red-brown, and off-white alongside the blacks and greys. The fourth look may introduce one of these accent colours. The garment construction continues the deconstructed, draped vocabulary.

**Look 05**
The fifth look is likely photographed mid-performance — a dancer in full step, the garment animated by the body's movement. The image captures what "Vicious" was arguing: that clothing exists in time and motion, not in the static pose of the conventional runway photograph.
