# mcqueen-ss99
## Alexander McQueen SS 1999 — "No. 13"

### Collection Overview
- **Designer:** Alexander McQueen
- **Season:** Spring/Summer 1999
- **Show Date:** September 1998
- **Venue:** Gatliff Road Warehouse, Victoria, London
- **Show Title:** "No. 13"

### Curatorial Notes
"No. 13" is widely regarded as the most theatrically ambitious show in the history of fashion. Staged in a raw industrial warehouse, it synthesised performance art, disability politics, and post-industrial anxiety into a sustained forty-minute act. The collection opened with a series of structured, anatomically constricting garments — cage-like constructions in lace and leather that referenced both Victorian mourning dress and orthopedic apparatus — before escalating toward its now-mythic finale.

The show's climax featured model Shalom Harlow standing on a slowly rotating wooden platform in a white strapless dress while two industrial robotic arms (borrowed from the automotive industry and reprogrammed by McQueen's team) sprayed her with black and yellow paint. The act collapsed the boundary between couture and manufacturing, body and machine, beauty and violation. It remains one of the most reproduced images in fashion history.

McQueen's recurring preoccupation with the female body as a site of both power and vulnerability was never more explicitly staged. The opening looks — austere, bone-pale, restrictive — function as the controlled premise from which the finale's controlled chaos detonates. The collection also notably featured double amputee athlete and model Aimee Mullins walking on hand-carved wooden prosthetic legs sculpted by McQueen himself.

### Target Looks 01–05

**Look 01**
Severe skirt suit with lace appliqué detailing. Anatomically tight bodice in ivory/ecru, structured to restrict the torso. High-collared or décolleté depending on version. Skirt falls below the knee in a pencil or A-line silhouette. The lace functions not as decoration but as a skin-map, tracing the body's contours aggressively. Sets the tone of constriction and control that governs the entire opening sequence.

**Look 02**
Continuation of the pale, structured suiting vocabulary. Likely a tailored jacket with exaggerated shoulder architecture over a narrow skirt or trouser. McQueen's tailoring in this period drew from his training at Anderson & Sheppard on Savile Row — the construction is impeccable beneath the conceptual surface. Look for visible boning or internal structure visible at seams.

**Look 03**
The palette begins to introduce darker tones or textural contrast — black lace over skin, or a shift from bone-white toward grey. McQueen frequently used the third look to introduce a tonal pivot. May include feather or fur trimming at cuffs or hem.

**Look 04**
Introduces Aimee Mullins in hand-carved wooden prosthetic legs. The look itself is likely a draped or bias-cut garment that ends above the knee to allow the prosthetics full visual prominence. This is one of the most documented looks in the show. Confirm model identity and exact garment construction against primary sources.

**Look 05**
Further development of the anatomical tailoring — possibly a coat or jacket with exaggerated back seaming that maps the spine. McQueen frequently constructed backs as the primary visual surface, inverting the runway convention of front-facing display.
