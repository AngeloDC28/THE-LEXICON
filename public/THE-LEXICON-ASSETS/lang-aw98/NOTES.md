## Helmut Lang AW 1998 — "Séance de Travail"

### Collection Overview
- **Designer:** Helmut Lang
- **Season:** Autumn/Winter 1998
- **Show Date:** March 1998 (or earlier — see note below)
- **Venue:** Digital broadcast via CD-ROM; also shown in Vienna (unconfirmed — verify against press materials)
- **Show Title:** "Séance de Travail" (Working Session)

### Curatorial Notes
Helmut Lang's AW 1998 "Séance de Travail" is one of the most historically consequential acts in fashion's institutional history, and it had almost nothing to do with the garments themselves. In March 1998, Lang made the decision to show his collection on CD-ROM — distributed digitally before the Paris fashion week schedule — rather than staging a conventional runway show. This was a deliberate disruption of the calendar's hierarchy, which placed Paris at the centre and required every other fashion city to show before it in order to preserve Paris's position as the defining authority.

By showing first, digitally, Lang effectively challenged the premise of the entire calendar system. He argued — through action rather than statement — that the physical geography of the shows was irrelevant in a networked world, and that the content of a collection did not require the institutional theatre of a runway presentation to be legitimate. The gesture was widely discussed and genuinely disrupted the calendar; it also anticipated, by nearly two decades, the digital fashion show experiments that became widespread after 2020.

The garments themselves were characteristically Lang: razor-sharp utilitarian tailoring in his signature palette of black, white, nude, and silver. Bondage-influenced construction details — straps, buckles, exposed hardware — appeared alongside impeccably precise tailoring. The utilitarian aesthetic was not austere in the ascetic sense but precise in the engineering sense: every seam was where it was for a reason, every strap served a structural function. Lang's work of this period was deeply influential on the subsequent development of minimalism in fashion and on the aesthetic of the early internet era.

The collection's title — "Séance de Travail" (Working Session) — reinforced the anti-theatrical premise: this is work, not spectacle.

### Target Looks 01–05

**Look 01**
The opening look of Lang's utilitarian vocabulary at its AW 1998 most distilled. A precisely tailored jacket or coat in black — every seam intentional, the silhouette close to the body but not body-conscious in the Mugler or McQueen sense. Structural details may include exposed hardware (buckles, D-rings), bondage-influenced straps, or visible seaming on the exterior. The palette is black, white, or nude.

**Look 02**
The second look develops the utilitarian register. Possibly a trouser-and-jacket combination in which the tailoring's precision is the sole ornament. Lang's tailoring of this period drew from sportswear construction methods — bonded seams, technical fabrics, nylon and synthetic blends at a luxury price point. The construction was visible and celebrated rather than hidden.

**Look 03**
Introduction of the bondage-influenced structural details more explicitly: a strap across the chest or shoulder that serves a real structural function (holding a layer in place) while reading as a reference to restraint. Lang's engagement with BDSM aesthetics was always functional rather than decorative — the hardware worked.

**Look 04**
Possible introduction of the nude or skin-tone palette against the black — a sheer or skin-coloured underlayer beneath a black structured outer layer, creating a layering effect that references undressing and dressing simultaneously.

**Look 05**
The fifth look establishes the collection's full register. By this point the utilitarian vocabulary, the bondage references, the precise tailoring, and the limited palette are fully legible. The fifth look may introduce a material or colour variation — white or silver — that provides contrast against the dominant black opening sequence.
