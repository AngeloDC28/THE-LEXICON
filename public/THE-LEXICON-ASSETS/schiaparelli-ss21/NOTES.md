### Curatorial Notes
The Schiaparelli SS 2021 couture collection was Daniel Roseberry's full declaration of his vision for the relaunched house — and it arrived at a moment of extraordinary cultural intensity, the January 2021 shows taking place against the backdrop of the global pandemic, political upheaval, and the renewed prominence of bodily autonomy as a political subject. Roseberry's collection addressed the body directly and unapologetically as a site of power, desire, and weaponised beauty.

The collection's central motif was the anatomical body made external and artificial: moulded leather breastplates and torso pieces that reproduced the human chest in golden, sculpted, trompe l'œil form. The breasts, the ribcage, the navel — anatomical landmarks were translated into gilded, hardened surface, converting the biological into the architectural and the sexual into the armorial. The references ranged from Elsa Schiaparelli's own surrealist engagement with the body (her lobster dress, her shoe hat, her lip sofa — all strategies of displacement and double meaning) to Renaissance armour, ancient Greek kouros sculpture, and fetish wear.

The trompe l'œil hardware — gold chain-link necklaces, brooches, and adornments that were in fact moulded, painted, or cast elements of the garment itself — extended the anatomical logic into the accessories. A necklace that is part of the dress; a brooch that is part of the jacket; the boundary between garment and jewellery dissolved. This was a direct continuation of Elsa Schiaparelli's own collaborations with artists including Salvador Dalí and Jean Cocteau, in which the distinction between clothing and object was systematically refused.

Roseberry's SS 2021 collection established his place in the history of the house beyond question and is one of the most significant couture debuts of the twenty-first century.

### Target Looks 01–05

**Look 01**
The collection's opening statement of the anatomical breastplate motif. A structured jacket or bodice incorporating a moulded leather or metal-look torso piece that reproduces the female chest in gilded, sculptural form. The breastplate reads simultaneously as armour and as a celebration of the body it covers. The surrounding garment — jacket, skirt, trouser — is likely in Schiaparelli's signature black or deep navy, providing a neutral field for the gilded anatomical surface.

**Look 02**
Second anatomical look with variation. The breastplate or torso element may appear in a different configuration — more abstracted, or applied to a different garment form. The trompe l'œil hardware (a chain necklace that is part of the garment, a brooch that is moulded into the lapel) begins to appear.

**Look 03**
A look that foregrounds the trompe l'œil hardware vocabulary more extensively. The garment's surface is populated with gilded cast elements — chain links, buttons, coins, anatomical fragments — that appear to be added jewellery but are in fact integrated couture components. The surrealist logic of Elsa Schiaparelli's original practice is directly invoked.

**Look 04**
Introduction of a colour variation — possibly a deeply saturated Schiaparelli pink (the house's signature colour, derived from the shocking pink Elsa introduced in 1937) against the gilded anatomical elements. The combination of the shocking pink and the gold creates the collection's most direct reference to the founder's aesthetic.

**Look 05**
A look that extends the collection's armorial logic toward its extreme: a full torso piece, or a garment in which the anatomical moulding extends beyond the chest to include the abdomen, the shoulders, or the back. The body fully weaponised and aestheticised.
